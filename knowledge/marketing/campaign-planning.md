# Campaign planning

A campaign is a set of connected content with one goal, one audience, and an end date.
Different from ongoing content, which has no finish line.

## When a campaign is worth it

- Promoting a specific loan program to a specific audience
- A Realtor workshop or homebuyer seminar
- A seasonal moment with a genuine hook
- A market shift creating a real, explainable opportunity
- Launching a new referral relationship

Not worth it: "we should do more marketing." That is cadence, not a campaign.

## Structure

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| **Goal** | What specific outcome, measured how? |
| **Audience** | Who exactly? "Everyone" is not an audience. |
| **Duration** | Start and end date. Campaigns end. |
| **Core message** | One sentence. If it takes three, it is two campaigns. |
| **Content pieces** | What goes out and when |
| **CTA** | The same one across the campaign |
| **Follow-up** | What happens to responders |
| **Measurement** | The number that says whether it worked |

## Standard arc

```
Week 1   Problem awareness    Name the problem the audience has
Week 2   Education            Explain how it actually works
Week 3   Proof                A scenario, no private detail
Week 4   Direct offer         The single explicit ask
Ongoing  Follow-up            Responders get a real conversation
```

The ask comes **last**, after three weeks of usefulness. Leading with it is why most
campaigns fail.

## Program-specific campaigns

For VA, FHA, first-time buyer, self-employed, DSCR, or renovation:

1. Name the audience's actual problem — not the program's features
2. Correct the most common misconception about it
3. Say what genuinely disqualifies people, honestly — this builds more trust than the
   upside does
4. Give a concrete next step
5. **Never** state eligibility rules as settled fact without a source and date — see
   `tl-guideline-research`

## Follow-up is where campaigns are won

Most campaigns generate responses that nobody works. Decide before launch:

- Who responds, and how fast
- What the second touch is
- When a lead is retired

A campaign with no follow-up plan is content with a deadline.

## Compliance

Program-specific and rate-related campaigns carry the highest exposure of any marketing
work. Everything in [`marketing-compliance.md`](marketing-compliance.md) applies, and the
whole campaign should be reviewed before the first piece goes out — not piece by piece.
