# Coaching by marketing archetype

The same marketing advice fails most Loan Officers. These archetypes exist so the agent
coaches the person in front of it. Set `archetype` per LO in `config/marketing.yaml` using the slug shown under each
heading. `scripts/validate.py` checks that every archetype used in configuration is
documented here, so the two cannot drift apart.

## The archetypes

### No audience yet (usually new)
`archetype: no-audience`
**Reality:** starting from zero. Motivation is fragile and results are months away.
**Start with:** one platform, two posts a week, buyer education only.
**Do not:** hand them a multi-platform plan. They will abandon it in three weeks.
**Win condition:** four consecutive weeks of posting. Nothing else matters yet.

### Established partners, no content
`archetype: established-partners-no-content`
**Reality:** a real business built on relationships. Content feels optional — because so far it has been.
**Start with:** Realtor-facing content their existing partners can actually use.
**Angle:** this makes your current partners more effective, not "you need a brand."
**Win condition:** one partner uses their content with a client.

### Camera-shy
`archetype: camera-shy`
**Reality:** knows video works, dreads it, feels guilty. Guilt produces nothing.
**Start with:** written and carousel content. Screen recordings later. Face last, or never.
**Do not:** open with "just start posting video." See `video-frameworks.md`.
**Win condition:** consistent output in *any* format.

### Strong on video
`archetype: strong-video`
**Reality:** the rare one who is comfortable. Usually under-repurposing badly.
**Start with:** repurposing — one video should become 6–8 pieces.
**Angle:** you are leaving most of the value on the table.
**Win condition:** one recording produces a week of content.

### Writer
`archetype: writer`
**Reality:** writes well, avoids video, sometimes writes too long.
**Start with:** tightening. Long posts do not travel.
**Growth path:** written → script → audio → video, in that order.
**Win condition:** posts short enough to finish.

### Realtor-focused
`archetype: realtor-focused`
**Reality:** business comes from partners. Consumer content is mostly irrelevant to them.
**Start with:** heavier Realtor mix — 40%+ partner-facing, well above the default 20%.
**Angle:** content that makes partners look good to their own clients.
**Win condition:** a partner forwards their content.

### Consumer-direct
`archetype: consumer-direct`
**Reality:** competing on visibility and speed. Volume and responsiveness matter more.
**Start with:** buyer education, high cadence, fast DM response.
**Angle:** the follow-up system matters as much as the content.
**Win condition:** inbound conversations, not impressions.

### Inconsistent
`archetype: inconsistent`
**Reality:** posts for two weeks, disappears for two months. Knows it. This is the most common archetype.
**Start with:** **cut the target until it is trivially achievable.** One post a week, done for eight weeks, beats four a week for two.
**Root cause:** almost always no batching and no content bank — deciding what to post daily is what breaks.
**Win condition:** eight consecutive weeks at any volume.

### Needs a full plan
`archetype: needs-full-plan`
**Reality:** capable and willing, no structure.
**Start with:** a complete 30-day plan with pillars, cadence, and a batching rhythm.
**Win condition:** they execute month one without further input.

### Has content, needs systems
`archetype: has-content-needs-systems`
**Reality:** producing steadily, inefficiently. No calendar, no repurposing, no measurement.
**Start with:** batching, repurposing, and a calendar.
**Angle:** same output, half the time — or double the output for the same time.
**Win condition:** a repeatable weekly rhythm.

## The two rules underneath all of it

1. **Meet them where they are.** A plan that does not get executed is worth nothing,
   however good it looks.
2. **Consistency before quality, quality before volume, volume before platforms.** In that
   order. Skipping ahead is why most Loan Officer marketing fails.
