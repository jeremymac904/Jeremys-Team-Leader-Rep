# Call to action

A CTA creates a useful next step. It does not apply pressure and it does not promise an
outcome.

## By audience

**Soft** — low commitment, builds the list
> "Message me your scenario." · "Send me the question you are stuck on." · "Comment
> CHECKLIST and I will send the starter list." · "Save this before you start house shopping."

**Buyer**
> "Message me PLAN if you want to know what to review before looking at homes."
> "Send me your price range and county and I will tell you what documents a review starts with."
> "If you were told no, send me what happened. The next step depends on the details."

**Realtor**
> "Realtors, send me a buyer scenario and I will tell you what I would verify before they write."
> "If you want cleaner pre-approval conversations for your buyers, let's compare notes."
> "Message me PARTNER and I will send my buyer-readiness checklist."

**Investor**
> "Message me INVEST with the property type and goal."
> "Send the purchase price, estimated rent, and exit strategy so we can identify what a review would need."

**Direct** — use sparingly, roughly 5% of content
> "Start a secure application when you are ready for a full review."
> "Book a quick scenario call."

## Why these work

Each asks for **information**, not a commitment. "Send me your scenario" is easy to say
yes to and starts a real conversation. "Call me for a free consultation" asks for a
meeting from someone who does not yet know if they need one.

The specific ones outperform the general ones. "Send me your price range and county" gets
answered; "reach out anytime" does not.

## Never

- CTAs that promise approval
- CTAs built on unsupported best-rate or savings claims
- CTAs implying universal qualification

## Disclosure

When a CTA points to loan options, qualification, pre-approval, or application, the
configured disclosure footer applies. See
[`marketing-compliance.md`](marketing-compliance.md).
