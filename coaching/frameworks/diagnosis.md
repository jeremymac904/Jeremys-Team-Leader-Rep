# Diagnosis before prescription

The most common coaching mistake is prescribing before diagnosing. An LO says "I need more
leads," the leader agrees, and three months later nothing has changed — because the problem
was never lead volume.

## Walk the funnel and find the first break

```
Conversations  →  Appointments set  →  Appointments held  →  Applications  →  Closings
```

Compute the step-to-step conversion at each stage. **The first stage where the ratio falls
apart is the problem.** Everything downstream of it is a symptom, and everything upstream of
it is fine no matter how it feels.

| Where it breaks | What it usually means | What NOT to fix |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough conversations | Activity problem. Blocks are not protected, or the list does not exist. | Don't work on scripts yet |
| Conversations → appointments | They are not asking. Or they are pitching instead of discovering. | Don't add more dials |
| Appointments set → held | Weak confirmation, or the appointment was never real. | Don't blame the leads |
| Appointments → applications | The consultation is not closing. Objection handling or urgency. | Don't add more appointments |
| Applications → closings | Structure, expectation-setting, or process. Often not a sales problem at all. | Don't coach sales skill |

## Say what it is NOT

Half the value of a diagnosis is ruling things out. "This is not a lead problem — you had 96
conversations. It is an asking problem" saves an LO from three months of chasing the wrong fix.

## Two people, same number, different problem

Two LOs both closed 2 units. One had 300 conversations and 4 applications. The other had 40
conversations and 3 applications. These are opposite problems:

- The first has a **conversion** problem. More activity makes it worse — more wasted effort.
- The second has an **activity** problem. Their conversion is good; there is simply not
  enough going in the top.

Coaching them the same way fails one of them.

## Then ask what changed

If someone's numbers dropped sharply against their own trailing average, the funnel is
usually not the answer. Ask what changed — personally, in their market, in their partner
mix — before diagnosing the business. Production drops are very often life events.

## Prescribe one thing

Once you know the stage, pick **one** change. Not three. An LO working on one thing for four
weeks beats an LO working on five things for one week, every time.
