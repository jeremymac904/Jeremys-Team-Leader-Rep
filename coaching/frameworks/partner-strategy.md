# Referral partner strategy

A good agent relationship is a renewable pipeline. The standard is simple and almost nobody
holds it: **one coffee or lunch per week, minimum.**

## Build the Target 25

Twenty-five agents you are deliberately working, drawn from:

- Agents you already have some relationship with
- Local producers doing volume in your market
- Agents you met at open houses or on transactions
- Agents serving the buyer profile you are best at

Twenty-five is the number because it is small enough to actually work and large enough to
survive attrition.

## Segment honestly

| Segment | Definition | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Producing** | Referred within 90 days | Protect it. Do not take it for granted. |
| **Warm** | Engaged, no referral yet | Find the test. What would make them send one file? |
| **Cold** | No contact in 60+ days | Re-engage deliberately, or retire it |
| **Stopped** | Used to refer, no longer does | **Highest priority.** Something happened. |
| **New** | Not yet tested | Weekly touch until they meet, test, or opt out |

The "stopped" category is where the money is, and it is the one everybody skips because it
is uncomfortable.

## Bring something specific

"Checking in" is not a value item. Real ones:

- A market update they can send to their own sphere
- Buyer education for their next open house
- A fast-turn commitment they can promise their clients
- Co-branded content with their name on it
- Solving one problem that keeps killing their deals

Ask what actually goes wrong in their transactions. The answer is usually specific,
solvable, and not about rate.

## The meeting

Ask about their business, where deals fall apart, what clients complain about, and what
support would make them look good to their own clients. Then shut up and listen. Bring the
solution to the next meeting, not this one.

## Handle objections without arguing

"I already have a lender" is not a no. Acknowledge it, ask what they like about that
relationship, and offer a low-risk test — one file, one pre-approval, one second opinion.
Then follow through fast enough that the difference is obvious.

## A compliance line you cannot cross

Anything of value given to a referral source raises **RESPA** questions. Marketing services
agreements, co-marketing splits, event sponsorships, gifts, and paid leads all have rules,
and the rules are not intuitive.

Education and genuinely free content are generally the safe ground. Anything that looks like
payment for referrals is not. When in doubt, ask compliance before you offer it, not after.
The AI will flag these; it will not clear them.
