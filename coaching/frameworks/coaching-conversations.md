# Coaching conversations

## The 30-minute one-on-one

The structure exists so the session cannot drift into a friendly catch-up that changes
nothing.

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Numbers** | Goal vs actual. What hit, what missed. No interpretation yet. |
| 5–10 | **Activity** | Were the blocks honored? Were theme days run? |
| 10–15 | **Skill** | Which specific behavior needs work — from the diagnosis, not from a list |
| 15–25 | **Drill** | Live rep. Actually say it out loud. |
| 25–30 | **Commit** | One number, one focus, one next step |

Then document it the same day. A week later you will not remember, and the next session
will start from nothing.

## Adapt to the level

| Level | Emphasis | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **New** | Activity volume and structure. Do not optimize conversion yet. | Encouraging, very specific, one change at a time |
| **Developing** | Consistency and conversion. Turn activity into appointments. | Direct, hold the standard, celebrate the streak |
| **Established** | Partner strategy and the weakest ratio | Peer-level. Challenge the plan, not the person |
| **Top producer** | Leverage, delegation, capacity — not more activity | Consultative. Earn the right to push |

Coaching a top producer to make more calls is how you lose a top producer.

## Ask instead of tell

An LO who reaches the diagnosis themselves will act on it. One who is told will comply for
about a week.

- "Walk me through Tuesday."
- "You had 96 conversations and set 1 appointment. What do you think is happening there?"
- "What did you say right before they said they'd think about it?"
- "What would have to be true for you to hit 8 next month?"
- "What is actually in the way?"

## The four hard conversations

### 1. Consistently missing the standard
Lead with the pattern, not the feeling. "Three weeks below 15 conversations. Before we talk
about how to fix it — what is actually going on?" Ask before prescribing. Then move to a
written plan, so it stops being a repeated verbal conversation neither of you enjoys.

### 2. Good activity, no results
This is a skill problem, and the LO usually knows it and is embarrassed. Say the effort is
real and visible, then get specific about where it breaks. The relief of a precise diagnosis
is enormous.

### 3. The top producer who ignores team process
Do not fight over the scorecard. Find out what the process costs them and whether it can be
made cheaper. If the answer is that they genuinely do not need it, decide consciously
whether to make an exception — and if you do, say out loud why, so it does not read as
favoritism to everyone else.

### 4. Someone who has quietly checked out
Almost always something outside work. Ask directly and kindly, and be prepared for the
answer to be that they are leaving, or should. Do not diagnose the funnel first.

## Where to stop

The moment a conversation becomes disciplinary, compensation-related, or a protected-class
matter, it stops being coaching. Involve HR. Do not have your AI draft it, and do not
freelance the documentation — get it right with a human.
