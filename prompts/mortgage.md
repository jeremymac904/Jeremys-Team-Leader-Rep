# Mortgage prompts

> **Read this first.** Everything in this file is for **education and training**. The agent
> is not the underwriter, not compliance, not your AUS, and not an authority on current
> agency guidelines. Guidelines and overlays change constantly, and an AI answering from
> memory will sound confident and be out of date.
>
> **Never** paste borrower PII — names, SSNs, account numbers, credit reports, income
> documents — into any AI tool. Borrower-specific questions go to underwriting.

---

## Teach a program to the team

> Explain [program] for training purposes. Cover the general rule, the common exceptions,
> and what is genuinely fact-specific. Separate agency guideline from investor overlay from
> lender-specific rule — my team confuses these constantly. Cite every source with its
> publication date and the date you retrieved it, and tell me to verify before relying on it.

---

## Build a cheat sheet

> Build a one-page cheat sheet my LOs can keep at their desk for [program]. Include the
> qualifying basics, the three things that most often kill these files, and the questions to
> ask a borrower up front. Mark anything time-sensitive that will need re-checking, and cite
> sources with dates.

---

## Structuring, in general terms

> Generic scenario for training, no real borrower: [describe the structure in general
> terms — no names, no identifying detail]. What are the general options for a file that
> looks like this, and what would an underwriter want to see? This is education for my
> team, not a decision on a live file.

---

## Compare programs for teaching

> Compare [program A] and [program B] for training purposes. When does each generally make
> more sense? What do LOs most often get wrong about the comparison? Cite sources and dates.
> Do not include rate or payment figures.

---

## Objection handling on product

> My LOs freeze when a borrower says "[objection]". Give me the structure of a good answer:
> what to acknowledge, what to ask, and how to reframe — without quoting a rate, promising
> approval, or disparaging anyone. Then role-play it with me at difficult level.

---

## What changed?

> I heard [program/agency] updated [something]. Find the authoritative current source, tell
> me what it actually says, give me the effective date, and tell me plainly if you cannot
> find a current source rather than answering from memory.

**If it cannot source it, it should say so.** An unsourced guideline answer is worse than
no answer.

---

## Where to stop

The agent must refuse, and route to a human, when a question involves:

- A specific borrower's credit, income, assets, or property
- Whether a specific file will be approved
- A rate, fee, APR, or payment quote
- Drafting a disclosure or a denial

Those are underwriting, pricing, and compliance functions. They are not AI functions.
