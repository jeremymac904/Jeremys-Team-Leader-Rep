# Referral partner prompts

*The agent never contacts a partner. Drafts are for you to send.*

## Build the Target 25

> I want to build a deliberate list of 25 agents to work. My market: [describe]. My best
> borrower profile: [describe]. What criteria should put an agent on this list, how do I
> find them, and how do I rank them so I know who gets weekly versus monthly attention?

---

## The one that stopped

> [Agent] used to send me [N] referrals a quarter and has sent none in [N] months. Last
> contact: [when]. What most likely happened, what should I ask, and how do I open the
> conversation without it sounding like I am chasing business? Assume a file went badly and
> nobody told me.

**Why this one matters:** it is the highest-value and most-avoided category in any partner
list.

---

## What to actually bring them

> [Agent] does [describe their business]. Their clients are [profile]. Give me five specific
> value items I could bring — things that make them look good to their own clients. No
> "check in," no generic market update. And flag anything that raises a RESPA question.

---

## Partner meeting prep

> Prep me for coffee with [agent]. History with us: [describe]. Give me what to bring, what
> to ask about their business, and one low-risk next step to propose. I want to leave with a
> commitment, not a nice conversation.

---

## "I already have a lender"

> Role-play. You are an agent who already has a lender you are happy with. Difficulty:
> difficult. I am trying to earn one test file. Push back the way a real agent would.

---

## Quarterly value plan

> For my top [N] partners, plan one useful thing per month next quarter. My specialties:
> [list]. My marketing capacity: [describe honestly]. Flag anything that needs compliance
> review before I offer it.

---

## Is this relationship worth keeping?

> [Agent]: [N] months of contact, [N] referrals, [N] closed. Time invested: [describe].
> Should I keep working this relationship, change the approach, or retire it? Give me the
> case for retiring it — I am probably being sentimental.
